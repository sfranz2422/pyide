The block world

Building in the block world

A program that imports mcpi builds in a 3D world of blocks, the way the Raspberry Pi's Minecraft lessons do. Most Pi lesson programs work here as they are. The world is kept with your project, so what you build is still there next time.

Block worlds come with assignments a teacher sets: on the class page, Add to class → Block world (Minecraft). Students open them from their class page like any assignment.

from mcpi.minecraft import Minecraft
from mcpi import block

mc = Minecraft.create()
mc.postToChat("Hello, world")
x, y, z = mc.player.getTilePos()
mc.setBlock(x + 2, y, z, block.STONE.id)

Moving about

Click the worldtake the mouse, to look about with it
W A S Dwalk
Spacejump; press it twice quickly (or F) to fly, then Space goes up and Shift down
1–9, or the mouse wheelchoose from the hotbar along the bottom
The sword (slot 1)clicking a block with it changes nothing: it's what mc.events.pollBlockHits() reports
Any other slotleft click breaks a block, right click places one
Tsay something in chat: mc.events.pollChatPosts() reads it
Escgive the mouse back. Your program keeps running: Stop ends it

The commands

mc is what Minecraft.create() gave you. Positions are blocks: x and z across, y up.

A program that keeps going needs time.sleep(0.1) in its loop, as on a Pi: it gives the world its turn to move. Stop reaches your program as a KeyboardInterrupt, so except KeyboardInterrupt: can tidy up, as Ctrl+C does on a Pi.

Wool colours

Wool's data is its colour: mc.setBlock(x, y, z, block.WOOL.id, 14) is red, and so is block.WOOL.withData(14).

Not quite a Pi

  • Water and lava stay where they're put, sand doesn't fall, and TNT doesn't explode.
  • There's one player: you. mc.getPlayerEntityIds() is [1].
  • The world is 256 blocks across each way (x and z from -128 to 127) and y runs from -64 to 63. Blocks set outside it are left out.
  • Nothing here is Minecraft's: the world, its pictures and the mcpi package are PyIDE's own.
